About the programme

Computer Networks and Data Communication is a discipline that studies the principles of computer networks; the ways of designing and building network systems at different levels from local to global; the data exchange between network components; and the development of network applications, web applications, big data processing and high-performance computing.

The programme aims to train engineers with the theoretical, mathematical and scientific foundations used in the computer networking and data communication industries. Students are equipped with basic and in-depth knowledge, the ability to analyse, design and propose solutions that meet the needs of an organisation or individual: network applications, mobile software, free and open-source software, web applications and services, the client-server model, grid computing, cloud computing, big data, parallel and distributed algorithms, and the Internet of Things.

Students are able to communicate in both general and specialised English; they can work independently or in teams, with professional ethics and good health, and have the capacity for research, creativity and entrepreneurship in the context of Industry 4.0.

Study plan by semester

The standard study plan of the Computer Networks and Data Communication programme spans 13 semesters over 4.5 years (three semesters a year), as set out in the programme specification applied from Cohort 51. Each semester heading gives its total credits; for electives, students choose courses up to that semester's elective credits. Semester 10 offers two tracks: 9 credits from track N1 (Network design and administration) or N2 (High-performance computing).
