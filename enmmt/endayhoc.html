Teaching, Learning and Assessment

How teaching and learning are organised and how student achievement is assessed, aligned with the learning outcomes of each programme.

Educational philosophy

The programme is designed and delivered according to Can Tho University's educational philosophy “Community – Comprehensiveness – Excellence” (resolution of the University Council, March 2023) and the philosophy of the College of Information and Communication Technology, “Thinking, Creativity, Learner-centredness”. It is communicated through the university portal, the student handbook, orientation week and academic advising meetings, and is put into practice as follows:

Community

Projects, essays and the graduation thesis address real problems of industry and society; the Company Internship places students full-time for 2–3 months in an organisation.

Comprehensiveness

The learning outcomes cover knowledge, skills, autonomy and responsibility, from science and society to in-depth specialisation, soft skills and professional ethics, mapped onto every course's outcomes.

Excellence

Cloud computing, information security, IoT and artificial intelligence are brought into the curriculum promptly; almost all foundation and specialised courses include practical work.

Teaching and learning activities

The programmes combine lectures with a substantial amount of practical work. The following learning activities are used across all three programmes:

Laboratory practice

Courses on computer networks, network management, network security and cloud computing are practised on the equipment and infrastructure of the Faculty's two laboratories.

Projects at increasing levels

A basic project in year three and an advanced project in year four gradually introduce students to research and to solving real-world problems.

Company internship

The final semester of the programme is dedicated to an internship — students work in a real professional environment before graduation.

Graduation thesis / project

Students carry out a topic under the supervision of an academic staff member and defend it before a committee.

Students of the high-quality programme take more than 60% of their courses in English and have the opportunity to join exchange and study programmes abroad.

Active learning

Foundation and specialised courses typically split their hours 50/50 between lecture rooms and computer labs. Lab topics and instructions are published in advance on the learning management system, together with virtualised lab environments that students can use from anywhere. Student assessment

Assessment is governed at three levels: the university's Academic Regulations (Decision 3266/QĐ-ĐHCT of 15 August 2024) and Regulations on archiving final examinations (Decision 3595/QĐ-ĐHCT of 6 July 2026); Faculty guidelines on grade appeals and on the conduct and assessment of graduation theses; and the detailed syllabus of each course.

  • Theory or theory-and-practice courses have at least two component marks and a compulsory final examination.
  • Methods, weightings and the course outcomes assessed are proposed by the lecturer, approved by the department and the Faculty, and published in the course syllabus and the online classroom from the first session.
  • Examination schedules are announced at least one week in advance; papers and answer keys are approved by the Faculty leadership.
  • Component marks filed with the scripts must match the published weightings; this is checked every semester.

A review of the current syllabi of 55 foundation and specialised courses found 11 assessment methods in two groups. Each learning outcome from PLO3 to PLO10 is measured by at least three methods, including at least two product- or performance-based methods. Across 37 courses with syllabi in both 2021 and 2026, the weight of multiple-choice tests fell from 34.9% to 22.0%.

Failed or outstanding credits

Failed credits above 50% of the semester's registration, or more than 24 outstanding credits.

Each semester the College office sends advisors the list of students to follow up. Advisors meet each student, record the causes, advice given, credits for the next semester and the student's commitment, countersigned by the Dean. In four review rounds from semester 2 of 2024–2025 to semester 3 of 2025–2026, 31 students were advised individually; 14 of 29 first-time warnings were cleared by the next round.

A student under academic warning may register for a limited number of credits only and should meet their academic advisor immediately to revise the study plan. Repeated warnings may lead to expulsion.

Feedback to students and appeals

Release of marks

Marks are visible to students as soon as the lecturer enters them in the academic system, the MyCTU app and CTU e-Learning. The university sets and publishes the mark-entry deadline for every semester.

Feedback on work

Criterion-by-criterion rubric comments on group assignments, weekly internship logs and committee feedback at the thesis defence.

Appeals

Submitted by form or e-mail to the Faculty; two lecturers re-mark; the whole process takes 7 days. The re-marked result may go up, stay the same or go down.

In the College's course surveys, the share of students agreeing that lecturers explain objectives, content, methods and assessment clearly rose from 81.7% (semester 1, 2023–2024) to 87.4% (semester 2, 2025–2026); satisfaction with lecturers' feedback on results rose from 79.6% to 84.1%.
