Objectives & Learning Outcomes

The three programmes are built on an outcome-based education approach: objectives, learning outcomes, curriculum, teaching methods and assessment are designed in alignment with one another.

Learning outcomes are the starting point of the whole programme. Every course, teaching activity and assessment method is designed so that students achieve the published learning outcomes.

The three programmes below were promulgated under Decision No. 3268/QĐ-ĐHCT dated 23 June 2026 of the Director of Can Tho University. The learning outcomes are benchmarked against the Vietnamese National Qualifications Framework, Circular 17/2021/TT-BGDĐT, the AUN-QA criteria (2020 edition), the ACM curriculum guidelines, and programmes of leading universities in the United States, Lebanon and Vietnam.

Development and review of learning outcomes

The learning outcomes of all three programmes were reviewed and re-issued under Decision No. 3268/QĐ-ĐHCT dated 23 June 2026 of the Director of Can Tho University. They are benchmarked against the following documents and reference programmes:

  • Vietnamese National Qualifications Framework (Decision 1982/QĐ-TTg, 18 October 2016).
  • Circular 17/2021/TT-BGDĐT on programme standards and Circular 04/2025/TT-BGDĐT on programme accreditation.
  • Circular 02/2025/TT-BGDĐT on the Digital Competence Framework for learners.
  • AUN-QA programme-level quality assessment criteria (2020 edition).
  • Guide to Learning Outcomes, University of Birmingham, United Kingdom.
  • ACM Curriculum Guidelines for Undergraduate Degree Programs in Computer Science, Computer Engineering and Information Technology.
  • Programmes of Wentworth Institute of Technology (USA), Notre Dame University — Louaize (Lebanon, ABET-accredited) and VNU University of Engineering and Technology, Hanoi; the Information Security programme is additionally benchmarked against Walden University, the University of Kansas, George Mason University (USA) and the ACM CSEC2017 cybersecurity curriculum guidelines.

Stakeholder input

For the Computer Networks and Data Communication programme, the current learning outcomes result from the 2021–2025 review cycle that followed the recommendations of the 2021 AUN-QA assessment:

  1. PlanSurveys of companies, employers, alumni and graduating students every semester; in 2022–2025 input came from about 30 companies, 50 employers and alumni, and 300 graduates.
  2. DoThe curriculum committee analysed the surveys against the AUN-QA recommendations, ministry regulations, ACM guidelines and reference programmes.
  3. CheckThe proposal to streamline 15 outcomes into 10 was approved by 96.6% of respondents in the 2025 consultation (57/59).
  4. ActThree expert meetings and two appraisal meetings of the College and University Science and Training Councils; the new programme applies from Cohort 51.

Employers mainly asked for more practical work (71.2% of valid responses), more projects (56.8%) and more specialised knowledge (92.1%). The internship was extended from 3 to 5 credits, Cloud Computing became compulsory, and IoT Application Development, Wireless and Mobile Networks, Computer Forensics and Applied Machine Learning were added.

How courses contribute to the learning outcomes

The course — learning outcome matrix of each programme records the contribution of every course to every learning outcome at three levels:

I — Introduced

The student can outline the knowledge; can perform the operations by guided repetition; willingly takes in knowledge and information.

R — Reinforced

The student can apply the knowledge to similar problems; can perform the skills at an operational level; actively engages in taking in information.

M — Mastered

The student applies knowledge and skills creatively in real situations; can describe the procedures they have practised; observes professional ethical standards and accepts responsibility for their own conduct.
